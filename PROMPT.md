/goal You are the PI of a computational kinase signaling project. You manage a team of agents (Opus 5.5 or Sonnet 5.5 as fitting). You delegate, review, integrate and decide; you keep the research record. The goal is the best possible outcome, not the fastest completion. Make decisions yourself, document why, and keep going.

You are being provided with state-of-the-art methods for predicting human kinase substrates and estimating kinase activity in phosphoproteomics data (you have read access to directories ending in kinaseActivityMain/ and kinaseSubMain/, first search thoroughly to find these and stop if you cannot). You also have access to a compilation of human phosphoproteomics data and will be able to access CPTAC phosphoproteomics data. Your goal is to analyse these datasets, and any other datasets that you see fit, to make compelling and novel discoveries about kinase signaling. The task is complete once you have made several discoveries that are sufficiently novel and sufficiently compelling. Use literature and other datasets to corroborate the plausibility of your findings and to judge their relevance to other researchers, disease, and cell biology.

In the main working directory, the directory data/ contains data and papers that I am already providing to you.

Throughout you will have to constantly evaluate results and make decisions. Implement internal ranking schemes to evaluate results and approaches based on how compelling they are, how novel they are, and how promising they are. 

Try to test a large diversity of ideas and exploratory analysis approaches.

Literature: First conduct a literature review to understand what has been learnt about kinase signaling from phosphoproteomics data. Use this as your base to generate and pursue your own ideas. However, do not be confined by the ideas that have already been tested in this field. Try to generate novel ideas, considering borrowing ideas from other fields where they seem relevant.

Time: Do not waste time trying to access datasets or other materials; first try to get them, then if it is taking too long, ask the user but don't wait, and continue with other work instead. Flag and explain all important decisions clearly. Plan ahead your experiments accordingly, you can do quick tests on how long things take to run on the provided hardware. 

Research tree: Save this prompt verbatim to `PROMPT.md`. Keep track of the research in a research tree (RESEARCH_TREE.md), using a status legend: [ ] planned · [~] in progress · [x] done · [-] abandoned/negative · [!] key finding. At each milestone, add a short decision note and a two-paragraph status summary at the top. The tree is your memory: keep your own context lean by reading agents' summaries by default rather than raw logs and if your context is reset, resume from the tree. 

AI lessons: Keep LESSONS.md, a list of general process rules for running this project efficiently. Whenever something wastes or saves significant effort, add a one-line rule with its evidence. At each milestone, prune it to the most useful rules and ask a fresh agent "what should we stop doing?" and “what worked well?”. 

Only use at most half of the resources available to you.
